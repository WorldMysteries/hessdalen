STUDY THE CASE

04 / METHOD

Fewer guesses.
More data.

The phenomenon has a long history of observations and instrument-based research. But the accumulation of data does not mean the origin has already been established.

MEASUREMENT · TESTING · HYPOTHESISSTATUS: OPEN

HOW IT IS STUDIED

An event must leave a trace

Instruments answer different questions.
None of them replaces context.

01

Optical recording

Cameras and photo documentation help establish how the light changed and where it was in the frame.

IMAGE
02

Spectral analysis

A spectrum can provide information about the light and its source — if the recording is of sufficient quality and correctly matched to the event.

LIGHT COMPOSITION
03

Radar and sensors

The 1984 campaign used radar and other measuring means; the set of instruments recorded different parameters.

MOTION · ENVIRONMENT

The key question is whether the readings of several instruments match the same light event in time and direction.

HYPOTHESES ARE NOT CONCLUSIONS

Several directions
for verification.

Physical and atmospheric models were proposed in scientific discussions. Below are generalised directions, not a list of proven causes.

01

HYPOTHESIS

Ionised gas

Plasma and dust-plasma models attempt to explain the glow and behaviour of some observations.

REQUIRES VERIFICATION BY DATA
02

HYPOTHESIS

Natural electrical processes

Geological and electrical processes in the environment were discussed. Such versions require independent measurements.

REQUIRES VERIFICATION BY DATA
03

TESTING ALTERNATIVES

An ordinary source or optics

Some reports may have an ordinary explanation: a light source, perspective, atmospheric conditions or features of the imaging.

TO BE RULED OUT IN EVERY CASE

Important: an explanation of one observation does not necessarily explain all the others. The scientific literature on the phenomenon has not arrived at a generally accepted model of its origin.
